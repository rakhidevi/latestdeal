Everyone loves a good sale, but not every "90% OFF" badge tells the truth. One of the most common tactics used during major sales events like the **Great Indian Festival** or **Big Billion Days** is price anchoring and artificial inflation.

## The "Price Anchoring" Trap

Retailers want you to feel a sense of urgency. To do this, they will often show a massively inflated "Original Price" (MRP) crossed out next to the sale price. 

**How it works:**
A pair of earphones normally sells for ₹1,499. The actual MRP listed on the box might be ₹4,999. During a sale, the seller might list the price as ₹1,299, but advertise it as **"74% OFF!"** based on the ₹4,999 MRP.

While you are still getting a small ₹200 discount, the 74% off claim is highly misleading.

## How to Protect Yourself

### 1. Check the Price History
This is exactly why we built the **Price History Chart** on LatestDeal. By looking at the historical data, you can see if a product typically sells for its "discounted" price year-round. If a TV has been selling for ₹45,000 for the last six months, a "sale" price of ₹44,990 is not a massive deal, regardless of the ₹65,000 MRP listed.

### 2. Ignore the Percentage, Look at the Absolute Price
Don't get caught up in the discount percentage. Ask yourself: "Is this product worth this specific amount of money to me?" If the absolute price is good, buy it. If not, skip it.

### 3. Beware of the Pre-Sale Price Bump
Another common tactic is for sellers to slowly raise the price of an item in the weeks leading up to a major sale. When the sale hits, they drop the price back to its normal level, creating the illusion of a discount. Again, checking historical price charts is the only reliable way to spot this behavior.

## The Bottom Line

Always use data, not marketing banners, to make your purchasing decisions. Before buying anything over ₹1,000, quickly search for it on LatestDeal to verify if the discount is genuine or just a marketing illusion.
